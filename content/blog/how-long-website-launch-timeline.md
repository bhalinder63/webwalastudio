"How long will this take?" is one of the first questions every business owner asks — and one of the most inconsistently answered questions in web design. Quotes range from "3 days" to "3 months" for work that often looks similar on paper. Here's what actually determines the timeline.

## The two things that actually control speed

**How fast you can provide content.** This is the single biggest variable, full stop. A business that has its logo, photos, and text ready on day one moves through the build dramatically faster than one that's still deciding on brand colors or hunting for product photos midway through. Agencies that quote unrealistically fast timelines without asking about your content readiness are setting an expectation they can't control.

**The agency's actual process, not just their promise.** "Fast" needs a defined workflow behind it — a fixed discovery step, a fixed design/build step, a fixed review step — or the timeline is just a hope, not a plan.

## What a real 7-day timeline looks like broken down

At Webwala Studio, our 7-day process looks like this once you've shared your content:

- **Days 1–2 — Discovery.** We confirm scope, structure, and any specific functionality (booking forms, e-commerce, portals) based on your business.
- **Days 3–6 — Design & Build.** The actual site gets built, styled, and tested across devices.
- **Day 6–7 — Go Live.** Final review, any last adjustments, and launch — plus a full handover with login credentials and a walkthrough.

This timeline assumes your content — logo, photos, key text — is ready to share at the start. If it isn't, we help you pull it together, which is common and doesn't derail the project, but does extend the timeline proportionally to how much needs to be created from scratch.

## What actually causes delays (and it's rarely the "build")

In our experience, delays almost never come from the actual design/development work — they come from:

- Waiting on content (photos, logos, copy) that keeps getting "sent tomorrow."
- Scope changing significantly mid-build (adding a feature that wasn't in the original brief).
- Slow feedback turnaround during the review stage.

None of these are unusual or unreasonable — they're just worth planning around if speed matters to you.

## What to ask any agency quoting you a timeline

- What exactly do you need from me, and by when, for that timeline to hold?
- What happens to the timeline if I'm a few days late providing something?
- Is the quoted timeline for a finished, launched site, or just a first draft?
