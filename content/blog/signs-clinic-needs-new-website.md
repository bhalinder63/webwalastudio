Most clinics don't decide to redo their website because of a dramatic failure — it's usually a slow accumulation of small frictions that quietly cost appointments. Here are five signs worth taking seriously.

## 1. It doesn't work properly on a phone

The overwhelming majority of patients searching for a doctor or clinic do it on their phone, often while dealing with the actual problem that's making them search. If your site requires pinch-zooming to read text, or buttons are too small to tap accurately, patients bounce to a competitor's listing instead of pushing through the friction.

## 2. There's no way to book an appointment without calling

A phone number is not a booking system — it's a barrier for anyone searching outside your clinic's working hours, which given search behavior, is a lot of your traffic. A simple appointment request form (even one that just requests a callback, if a live booking calendar isn't feasible yet) converts far more visitors than "call us."

## 3. Doctor information is missing or outdated

Patients want to know who they'd actually be seeing — qualifications, specialization, and photo — before they book. A clinic site that only has a generic "our doctors" paragraph without individual profiles is leaving a trust gap open that a competitor's site will close instead.

## 4. There's no social proof anywhere

Patient trust is the entire currency of a medical practice's marketing, and a website with zero testimonials or reviews reads as either new or unproven — even if the clinic has been operating for fifteen years and has plenty of happy patients who simply were never asked. A feedback section, even a modest one, closes this gap.

## 5. It looks and loads like it's from 2015

Design dates a site faster than most people expect, and an outdated-looking clinic website subtly signals an outdated practice — again, whether or not that's true. Slow load times compound this: patients researching a clinic while dealing with a health concern have very little patience for a page that takes eight seconds to appear.
