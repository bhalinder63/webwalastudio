This question comes up constantly from CA firms, consultants, and small retailers: "should my website have GST billing built in?" The honest answer is that most small business websites shouldn't — but a specific few genuinely need something adjacent to it. Here's how to tell which category you're in.

## When you don't need billing on your website

If your business takes orders or bookings and then invoices separately through Tally, Zoho Books, or a CA-managed system, your website doesn't need to duplicate that functionality. What it *does* need is:

- A clear services/pricing breakdown, so prospective clients know roughly what to expect before they contact you.
- A lead capture form that gets the right information (name, requirement, contact details) into your existing workflow.
- GST/compliance information as *content*, not functionality — for CA and accounting firms specifically, a resource page explaining GST filing deadlines or compliance requirements builds credibility and gives you something to rank for beyond your firm's name.

Building actual invoicing functionality you don't need adds cost and maintenance burden for no real benefit — it's the website equivalent of over-engineering.

## When you genuinely need more than a lead form

If you're running e-commerce — actually selling products through the site rather than generating leads — GST-compliant invoicing does need to be part of the checkout flow, since you're issuing real tax invoices to real customers at the point of sale. This is a legitimate e-commerce build requirement, not an optional add-on, and it should be planned into the site from the start rather than bolted on afterward.

## The middle ground: secure document handling

CA and accounting firms have a specific real need that isn't billing, but often gets confused with it: secure document exchange during tax season. Clients need to send you PANs, Form 16s, and financial statements without emailing sensitive documents back and forth insecurely. A **secure document upload portal** solves this directly, without needing to build actual billing software into your marketing website.

## What we'd actually recommend, by business type

- **Consultants and service businesses**: a clear fee/service breakdown page + lead form. No billing functionality needed.
- **CA & accounting firms**: a secure document upload portal, tax appointment booking, and a GST/compliance resource section — see our [CA & accounting firm website page](/services/ca-accounting-firms) for the full breakdown.
